Last month we wrote that AI agents now hold credentials, and that the controls any firm would insist on for a human administrator usually go unapplied to agents. Our advice was to give every agent its own identity, scope its access to the minimum it needs, and log what it does.

Our partner Zero Networks has now built that into its platform. Its AI capabilities apply the same identity-based microsegmentation it already uses for users, servers and workstations to AI services, agents and models.

There are five parts to it.

**SaaS AI control.** Governs which cloud AI services users and devices on your network can reach, and enforces it automatically. Approved tools work, and unapproved ones are blocked at the network layer rather than by a policy document nobody reads.

**AI agent control.** Treats every AI agent as a process with its own identity, and enforces least-privilege boundaries on what it can connect to. An agent built to read the ticketing system can’t also browse your file servers.

**LLM protection.** Segments model infrastructure so that only authorised systems can reach it. In Zero Networks’ words: “You can’t tamper with what you can’t reach.”

**AI lateral movement control.** Removes the network paths that would let a compromised user, system or agent move from where it is to where your critical data sits. This matters most for the prompt injection risk we described last month: an agent tricked into acting on hidden instructions can only reach what its segment allows.

**AI-powered compliance and risk engine.** Lets you ask questions about live network activity in plain language and returns dynamic risk scores mapped to NIS2 and CIS controls, flagging critical gaps as they appear.

The point isn’t the AI label. Most firms adopting agents are granting autonomy before defining boundaries, and this is a way to enforce the boundaries at the network layer, without redesigning the network and without depending on each agent behaving as intended.

For Singapore financial institutions, MAS’s Guidelines on AI Risk Management are still to be finalised, but the direction is already clear: know where AI runs in your environment, and control what it can touch. Segmentation answers the second half, and the visibility it gives you helps with the first.

We deploy and support Zero Networks in Singapore. If you’re piloting AI agents or running models on your own infrastructure, we can show you what they can reach today, and what they would reach once segmented.
