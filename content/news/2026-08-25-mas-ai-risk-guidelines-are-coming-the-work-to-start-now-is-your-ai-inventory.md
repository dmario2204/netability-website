MAS issued its consultation paper on Guidelines for AI Risk Management in November 2025. The consultation closed on 31 January 2026, and this month MAS Chairman and Deputy Prime Minister Gan Kim Yong said the Guidelines will be finalised soon.

When they are issued, financial institutions get a proposed twelve-month transition period. That sounds generous. For most firms it won't be.

The Guidelines apply to every MAS-regulated financial institution and cover four areas: AI governance and oversight, risk management systems and processes, controls across the AI lifecycle, and the capability to run all of it. Boards and senior management are given explicit responsibilities — setting risk appetite, approving frameworks, and holding enough AI literacy to meaningfully challenge what they approve.

The requirement that takes longest is the simplest to state: maintain an accurate, current inventory of where AI is used across your organisation, with a structured assessment of how material each use is.

Most firms underestimate this. AI is already in your environment whether you procured it deliberately or not — Copilot across Microsoft 365, AI features switched on by default in SaaS you already licence, models embedded inside vendor products. An inventory that captures only the AI projects you formally approved will not survive scrutiny.

The transition clock hasn't started yet. That is precisely why this is the moment to build the inventory — while it's preparation rather than remediation.
