FAA-N16 (Amendment) 2025 took effect on 29 December 2025. Licensed and exempt financial advisers must audio-record pre-transaction call-backs to Selected Clients and to clients of Selected Representatives, and the Notice now prescribes what those call-backs have to cover.

Nine months on, most firms we speak to have solved the recording part. They bought a platform, switched it on, and the calls are being captured. The requirements that get missed sit on either side of that.

The first is anti-circumvention. The Notice allows a non-recorded call-back only in limited circumstances, and one of the conditions is that the firm has established and implemented controls to mitigate the risk of supervisors, non-sales staff or third-party service providers circumventing the audio recording. That is a technical control, not a policy statement. If a representative can step out of the recorded channel onto a personal mobile or a consumer messaging app and complete the same conversation, the control does not exist no matter what the handbook says.

The second is retrieval. On a client's request, the firm must provide a copy of the audio recording or the full transcript, or the summary document where one applies. MAS has been explicit that firms should not add friction to this: no charging the client, no requiring them to come to your premises to listen. Recordings and summary documents must be retained for at least five years, alongside records of the processes used for the pre-transaction checks themselves.

Here is the test worth running this month. Pick a call from four months ago. Have someone who is not the system administrator locate it, confirm the metadata, produce a transcript, and package it in a form you could hand a client the same day. If that takes a week, or needs the vendor's help, or produces a file nobody can open, you have a finding waiting to happen.

Recording was the project. Producing the record on demand is the obligation.
