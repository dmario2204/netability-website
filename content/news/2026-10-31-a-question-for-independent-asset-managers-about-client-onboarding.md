For more than 16 years we have supported independent wealth managers in Singapore: their IT, their security and, increasingly, the compliance evidence that sits behind a CMS licence. In that time one topic has come up more than any other, and we would like to put it to the industry directly.

How does a new client actually get onboarded at your firm?

We don’t mean the policy, we mean the workflow. Where does the KYC pack arrive, who checks it, where does it end up, and how would you find it again?

In many firms the honest answer looks something like this. Documents arrive by email and are saved to a shared drive, sometimes more than once. Screening results sit in a separate tool or a PDF. Risk ratings live in a spreadsheet. Each custodian bank wants its own forms, and periodic reviews are tracked in someone’s calendar. It works until a relationship manager leaves, an inspection lands, or a client asks what personal data you hold about them.

None of the obligations are new. They include customer due diligence, ongoing monitoring and periodic reviews under MAS AML/CFT requirements, and keeping records for at least five years after a relationship ends. PDPA governs how client data is stored, accessed and eventually deleted, and MAS expects access control and audit trails on any system holding customer information. Each is manageable on its own. Spread across inboxes, folders and spreadsheets, together they become hard to evidence.

So we are asking. If you run or work at an independent wealth manager, external asset manager or family office in Singapore, we would value 20 minutes to hear how onboarding really works at your firm: what is manual, what breaks, and what you would change if you could.

This is a listening exercise first. Everyone who takes part will receive an anonymised summary of what we hear across the industry, so you can see how your process compares with your peers’.
